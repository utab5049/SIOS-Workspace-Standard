## 1. Purpose

KSM-001 is the candidate output artifact for RQ-600 ("How can validated knowledge become a reusable standard?"), as specified in RQD-001 §4.2.

This draft addresses two of RQ-600's five sub-questions — RQ-620 (specification development) and RQ-630 (governance) — where the pilot corpus currently has Approved evidence. It leaves RQ-610 (canonicalization), RQ-640 (versioning), and RQ-650 (traceability, partially covered) explicitly open.

*Note on RQ-650:* EV-000003 does bear on traceability, but its Approved claim is narrower than "how traceability is achieved and preserved" in general — it documents one specific traceability failure mode (unregistered external dependencies), not a full account of RQ-650. It is cited under §4.2 below accordingly, scoped to that finding.

## 4. Conceptual Model (Evidence-Grounded)

### 4.1 Finding 1 — Dependency Registration is a Distinct Standardization Concern

**Source:** EV-000003 (Approved)

**Statement:** A specification (in the case observed: CSL-001) can validly *cite* an external standard as normative (RFC 2119, RFC 8174) while that external standard remains **unregistered** in the citing project's own artifact registry (MAI-001), for an extended period, without the citing specification's own status (Frozen) being affected.

**Implication for RQ-620/RQ-650:** "Specification development" and "traceability" are not automatically coupled. A specification can be internally well-formed (CSL-001 is Frozen, internally consistent, and — per the confirmatory sub-finding in EV-000003 — already behaviorally compliant with what RFC 8174 requires) while its *external dependency bookkeeping* lags behind. This is a distinct failure mode from either (a) citing something incorrectly or (b) being internally inconsistent.

**Conceptual consequence:** A standardization framework needs at least two independent tracking mechanisms — one for the semantic correctness of what is cited, and one for the registry-completeness of the citation itself. EV-000003 provides evidence for the second; the pilot corpus has no evidence yet (Approved or otherwise) speaking to the first.

### 4.2 Finding 2 — At Least Two Distinct Standards-Governance Models Exist, With Different Legitimacy Bases

**Source:** EV-000004 (Approved)

**Statement:** The corpus documents one governance model (NRC 1995's account of voluntary consensus standards: multi-stakeholder drafting, mandatory response to negative votes) as a legitimacy mechanism aimed at broad external adoption. This is structurally distinct from a Board-approval model (as AERA-001F documents for SIOS itself), which does not use negative-vote-response as its legitimacy mechanism.

**Implication for RQ-630:** "Governance" in RQ-600's sense is not a single variable but at minimum involves a choice of *legitimacy mechanism*, and that choice plausibly correlates with the standard's intended breadth of external adoption (per the NRC source's own framing, though this correlation itself is not yet independently evidenced in this corpus — flagged as a testable claim, not an established one).

**Conceptual consequence:** This finding does **not** recommend SIOS change its governance model. EV-000004's own Context Summary and the independent review both explicitly frame this as an observation for awareness, not a defect claim. A future evidence-gathering round specifically on RQ-630 (with more than one source) would be needed before any prescriptive claim could be made here.
