## B.1 — Source Excerpts

**Source 1 — Gruber, T.R. (1993), "A translation approach to portable ontology specifications," *Knowledge Acquisition*, 5(2).**
> Defines ontology as a specification of a representational vocabulary for a shared domain of discourse — definitions of classes, relations, functions, and other objects.

**Source 2 — Studer, R., Benjamins, V.R., Fensel, D. (1998), "Knowledge Engineering: Principles and Methods," *Data & Knowledge Engineering*, 25(1-2).**
> States its working definition (§6.1): an ontology is a formal, explicit specification of a shared conceptualisation — citing this definition to two prior works: Gruber's 1995 paper "Towards Principles for the Design of Ontologies used for Knowledge Sharing," and Borst's 1997 PhD thesis "Construction of Engineering Ontologies."

**Source 3 — Salatino, A. et al. (2024), "A survey of knowledge organization systems of research fields," *Quantitative Science Studies*.**
> Defines ontology, from an information-technology perspective, as a formal and explicit description of domain knowledge that categorizes things by their essential qualities — attributing this definition directly to Gruber (1993).

**Source 4 — Klein, M., Fensel, D. (2001), "Ontology versioning on the Semantic Web," *SWWS Proceedings*.**
> Adopts Gruber's definition — "an ontology is a specification of a shared conceptualisation of a domain" — as the paper's own working definition, citing it directly.
