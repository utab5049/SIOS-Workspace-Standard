## P2.2 — Axis-Independence Question: Genuinely Still Open (Documented, Not Forced)

**Search conducted:** targeted queries cross-referencing named OEMs (METHONTOLOGY, Uschold & King, Grüninger-Fox, OTKM, FMCLGO) across both axis definitions.

**What was found:**
- SRC-000003 explicitly classifies METHONTOLOGY, Uschold & King, OTKM, and FMCLGO as **Non-Collaborative** (full text confirms this directly, verbatim category assignment).
- SRC-000004 explicitly classifies Grüninger-Fox as **Foundation** and FAO-based methods as **Derivative**.
- **Grüninger-Fox appears in both papers' methodology lists — but SRC-000003 does not classify it on the stakeholder-involvement axis in the text retrieved, and SRC-000004 does not classify it on the Collaborative/Non-Collaborative axis at all.**

**Honest conclusion:** No single source in this corpus cross-tabulates both axes for the same methodology. The one methodology (Grüninger-Fox) that appears in both papers is classified on only one axis by each — there is no direct overlap point to confirm or deny axis-independence from.

**Why this is not being forced to a synthesized answer:** Combining SRC-000003's silence on Grüninger-Fox's collaboration-mode with SRC-000004's silence on METHONTOLOGY's lineage, and guessing at the missing cells, would be exactly the kind of "researcher interpretation" OUM-001 prohibits at the observation stage and EUM-001 requires be explicitly flagged as interpretation, not extraction, if done at all. **The correct, disciplined answer is: this remains an open question**, and KOM-001 v0.2's existing explicit flag on this point ("independence assumption... not established") stands, unchanged, as accurate.

**What would actually resolve this:** A source that builds an explicit comparison table with both axes as columns — this is a specific, findable thing to search for in a future session, not a synthesis to attempt now with incomplete data.

**P2.2 Status: Complete as "confirmed still open, with a specific and now much more precise description of what's missing" — a legitimate research outcome.**
