## Correction to EV-000004

**Previous claim (from abstract-level extraction):** contrasted SIOS's Board-approval model against a single "consensus" standards category.

**Corrected, sharper claim:** SIOS's Board-approval governance model (AERA-001F §6.3 — Compatible/Minor changes decided by Architecture Board alone) does not map cleanly onto *any single one* of NRC's three categories. It has features of **all three simultaneously**:

- Like *consensus standards*, it involves a defined body reviewing proposals (though narrower than "private firms, technical experts, customers, and other interested parties" — SIOS's Board is not described as multi-stakeholder in that sense).
- Like *de facto standards*, adoption currently happens simply because SIOS's Frozen documents are what exists — there is no competing "market" of alternative SIOS specifications to converge against or away from.
- Like *government/regulatory standards*, a single designated authority (the Board) can set a requirement without external ratification, similar in *structure* (not force of law) to how a regulator mandates a standard.

**Revised Evidence Content for EV-000004:**
**Claim:** "SIOS's governance model, as currently specified, does not correspond to any single one of the three standards-development archetypes documented in NRC (1995); it most structurally resembles the regulatory/mandated archetype (a single designated authority decides) more than the multi-stakeholder consensus archetype, despite SIOS's own stated commitment to 'transparent governance' and 'public review' (SIOS-MAN-001, Constitutional Commitments) — language that more naturally describes the consensus archetype."
**Support Level:** E3 (now grounded in a source-provided taxonomy with three defined categories, not a single vague comparison)
**Confidence:** High (upgraded from Medium)

**This is a more pointed finding than the original EV-000004** — it's no longer just "these are different models," it's "SIOS's own stated aspirational language (transparent governance, public review) is in some tension with the structural archetype its actual approval mechanism most resembles." This is worth flagging to SIOS's maintainers as a genuine, if minor, aspiration-vs-mechanism gap — not a crisis, but a real observation about internal consistency between SIOS-MAN-001's rhetoric and AERA-001F's mechanics.
